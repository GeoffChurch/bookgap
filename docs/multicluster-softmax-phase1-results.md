## Notes on metrics

- **jaccard_old_to_new_mean**: mean over old basins of max-Jaccard against any new basin under this rule.
- **jaccard_new_to_old_mean**: reverse direction.
- **real_gap_count_new** uses a minimal label proxy: any non-book-anchored basin counts as real-gap. The richer tiering (inherited-strong/moderate/weak via gap_multi_scale_coverage) is out of Phase 1 scope.
- **top20_only_new_count**: number of new top-20 basins (by direct softmax effective_uncovered readout) absent from the old top-20 (by Phase-3 effective_uncovered).
- **achieved_P_last**: realized perplexity at the final tau-tuning step; should be close to P_target.

## Synthesis

The 12 configs × 3 crisp rules (36 rows above) tell a remarkably consistent story.

**1. Formulation alone produces materially different basins.** Mean
best-match Jaccard (old → new) ranges 0.146–0.172 across all one-shot
configs, with 422/423 old basins below the 0.80 threshold. Per the spec's
decision criterion (≥20% of old basins with Jaccard < 0.80), this clears
the bar by an order of magnitude (~99.8% of old basins are far from any
new basin). Even accounting for size mismatch — old basins are
tau_supp≥0.5-filtered (~80 members each) while new argmax-rule basins
average ~210 members (88460 / 423) — the achievable Jaccard ceiling
under perfect old-side recall would be ~0.4. Observed 0.15 means roughly
half of each old basin's members end up in some other new basin. The
softmax membership formulation places articles into different basins
than the per-seed pairwise posterior does.

**2. Iteration amplifies divergence and confirms diffusion / runaway.**
All 6 iterated configs hit max_iters=20 without converging
(final_delta 0.75–0.99, where 1.0 = fully scrambled membership). The
iterated mean Jaccard drops further to 0.10–0.11. The parent design
doc's Variant A "diffusion / runaway in book-poor regions" prediction
is clearly visible — but Variant B fails too. The ∅-regularization
does not stabilize iteration as hoped, likely because the basin-only
slice of M is fed back into the next E-step (see "Caveats" below);
mass that escapes to ∅ is not retained as a competitor. A faithful
Phase 2 lockstep EM would need to either (a) keep ∅ active across
iterations, or (b) hold book-anchored basin centroids fixed (the
"fixed competitors" framing in the parent design doc).

**3. Variant A vs Variant B is essentially indistinguishable in
Phase 1.** Mean Jaccard differs by < 0.005 between A and B in every
(P, iter_mode, rule) cell. The marginal-density ∅ class barely changes
the basin set in the one-shot regime. This is consistent with the kernel
geometry: each article's K̃_∅(a) is comparable in scale to the kNN
basin K̃ values, so most articles assign substantial probability to a
basin even in Variant B.

**4. Perplexity target sweep is mostly invariant.** P=1.5/2.0/3.0 vary
the achieved softmax sharpness (effective rank) but barely change
basin identity (Jaccard mean varies <0.03 across P). The membership
distribution flattens with P, but the argmax basin per article is
largely stable.

**5. Crisp-support rule sweep is also nearly invariant.** argmax,
p > 0.5, and confidence-margin produce basin sets with mean Jaccard
within 0.025 of each other in every cell. The crisp-rule choice is a
second-order effect.

**6. Top-20 leaderboard is fully disjoint.** Across all 36
(config, rule) cells, the symmetric difference of the top-20 basins
(ranked by `effective_uncovered_articles`, which is computed via the
direct softmax readout for new vs the Phase-3 multi-scale-coverage
posterior for old) is 20 — i.e., zero overlap. None of the new
top-20 "most uncovered" basins were in the old top-20. This is
partly attributable to the formula difference flagged in the spec
(direct softmax readout vs Phase-3 cross-basin posterior), but also
reflects that the basin sets themselves are disjoint.

### Refined verdict (post-hoc semantic spot-check, 2026-05-08)

A semantic check of basin contents — top article titles for high-recall
vs low-recall old basins, plus the three largest new basins — reframes
the Jaccard 0.15 number.

**The new pipeline is mostly expansion, not scrambling.** Per-old-basin
recall against best-match new basin:

- mean recall = 0.65, median = 0.72
- recall ≥ 0.50 for 70.2% of old basins (297/423)
- recall ≥ 0.90 for 31.2% of old basins (132/423)

Precision is correspondingly low (mean 0.19) because the new basin is
~7× larger on average — the new pipeline's argmax rule **partitions
every article**, while the old pipeline's `tau_supp ≥ 0.5` filter only
claims 21,009 / 88,460 articles (~24%). New basin sizes range up to
2698; old basins cap at 210.

**Semantically, the largest new basins are coherent broad topics, not
garbage:**

- new rank 102, n=2698 → computational / formal linguistics
- new rank 13,  n=1648 → corpus linguistics
- new rank 25,  n=1255 → Japanese/Korean linguistics

**For low-recall old basins (the ~30% where members scatter), the
best-match new basin is topically adjacent but framed differently.**
Sample inspection:

| old | old topic | new | new topic | recall |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| rank 1 | LLM reasoning + dynamic programming | rank 1 | combinatorial optimization, theorem proving | 0.04 |
| rank 3 | generation + diffusion models | rank 3 | multimodal MT | 0.09 |
| rank 2 | multimodal LLM reasoning | rank 2 | vision-to-language | 0.10 |

These aren't "wrong" reassignments — they're alternative topical
groupings of related material.

**What this means for the Phase 2 verdict.** The two pipelines aren't
producing fundamentally disjoint basin sets; they're producing the
same corpus partitioned at different granularities:

- **Old**: narrow tight seeds (~80 articles each), most articles
  unassigned. Each basin defined by a seed's pairwise posterior.
- **New**: broad natural partition (~210 average, up to 2698), every
  article assigned to its softmax-nearest basin. Each basin defined
  by its centroid in embedding space.

Phase 2 is still justified — these are different models and the
choice between "narrow seeds" vs "complete partition" is a real
design decision, not a numerical artifact. But the right Phase 2
question is **"what basin granularity do we want?"**, not "how do
we make the formulations agree?". The Phase 2 spec should make this
an explicit design choice: keep `tau_supp` as a post-hoc filter on
the multi-cluster softmax (recovering the narrow-seeds behavior),
or commit to the partition behavior (the design doc's vision), or
something tunable in between.

### Phase 1 addendum: --fix-book-anchored experiment (2026-05-08)

The Phase 2 design note "hold book-anchored basin centroids fixed
during iteration" was tested via a `--fix-book-anchored` flag added
to `run_softmax_reassign`. Result: **the fix does not stabilize
iteration.** All 6 iterated configs (variant × P) still hit
`max_iters=20` with final_delta ~0.93–1.00, in some cases slightly
worse than the unanchored baseline:

| config | final_delta no_fix | final_delta fixedBA |
|---|---|---|
| variantA P=1.5 | 0.997 | 0.991 |
| variantA P=2.0 | 0.972 | 0.981 |
| variantA P=3.0 | 0.788 | **0.926** |
| variantB P=1.5 | 0.987 | 0.995 |
| variantB P=2.0 | 0.973 | 0.979 |
| variantB P=3.0 | 0.752 | **0.928** |

T10181 has only 63 / 423 book-anchored basins (~15%). Holding those
centroids fixed leaves 360 non-book-anchored basins free to drift,
which is enough to keep the system in steady-state scrambling. The
P=3.0 deltas getting worse with the fix is consistent with
book-anchored centroids previously moving toward better positions
during EM that are now denied.

**Refuted Phase 2 design note:** "hold book-anchored basin centroids
fixed" is not sufficient. The convergence issue is more fundamental.

**Updated Phase 2 design notes** (the underlying problem is more
structural than this single fix):

1. The basin-granularity decision (narrow seeds vs full partition)
   is still the most important Phase 2 question — *unchanged*.
2. ∅ is not the bug — Variant B already includes the marginal
   density in every iteration via a per-topic constant — *removed*.
3. Holding book-anchored centroids fixed does not stabilize
   iteration — *refuted by this experiment*.
4. **Possible alternatives for Phase 2 to test:**
   (a) hold *every* basin's core (M0 nonzero) members fixed across
   iterations — anchor every centroid to its soft-EM origin while
   allowing softmax to expand the periphery;
   (b) damped updates (M_iter ← α·M_softmax + (1-α)·M_iter for
   α<1) to slow centroid drift;
   (c) accept that pure unsupervised lockstep EM doesn't converge
   in this formulation and ship the one-shot variant only.

The one-shot variant is robust and converges trivially. If Phase 2
ships one-shot with a tunable post-hoc support filter, the
narrow-vs-broad granularity question becomes the user's choice
without needing to solve iteration stability.

### Caveats on the readout

- **eff_rank_delta numbers (~421) are inflated** by an artifact: most
  T10181 articles have no soft-EM tau_supp-supported membership, so
  their normalized old distribution is filled with a uniform fallback
  (effective rank = N_basins = 423). The new pipeline assigns every
  article via softmax (effective rank ~1.5–3 by construction). The
  ~421 delta says "old assigns most articles to nothing; new assigns
  to one or two basins" — a real model difference but not a
  meaningful "softmax sharpens" signal.
- **real_gap_count comparison is not apples-to-apples.** The new
  pipeline uses a simple "non-book-anchored = real-gap" proxy. The
  old pipeline's strict v2 label criterion considers cross-basin
  inheritance via `gap_multi_scale_coverage` and reports 0 real-gap
  basins on T10181 (every basin had some inheritance). Re-running
  Phase-3 enrichment on the new basin set would let us compare
  apples to apples; out of Phase 1 scope.
- **Variant B iteration drops the ∅ column** when feeding M back to
  the next E-step. This is documented in the implementation but
  contributes to variant B not behaving differently from variant A
  in iterated mode. A Phase 2 implementation should keep ∅ active
  across iterations.

## Phase 2 verdict

**Phase 2 justified by formulation.** The one-shot multi-cluster
softmax already produces basins that differ materially from the
current per-seed soft-EM pipeline (Jaccard ~0.15 mean, 99.8% of old
basins below 0.80). Iteration amplifies but does not qualitatively
change this — it makes the basins even more different, not closer.

The supporting evidence is robust across all sweeps: 12 configs ×
3 crisp rules all agree; the only meaningful axes that move Jaccard
at all are oneshot-vs-iterated (~0.05 swing) and crisp rule
(~0.025 swing). Variant and perplexity are second-order.

This means the multi-cluster softmax formulation is doing real
work — it is not just a re-parameterization of the existing pipeline.
Phase 2 (lockstep EM restructure) should be pursued. Two design notes
for the Phase 2 implementer, derived from this run:

1. **Keep ∅ active across iterations** in Variant B — currently it's
   dropped when M is fed back, which is why Variant B doesn't
   stabilize iteration here.
2. **Hold book-anchored basin centroids fixed** during EM (the parent
   design doc's "fixed competitors" framing). Without this, both
   variants drift in book-poor regions (variant A diffuses, variant B
   doesn't have its ∅ regularization carried through). This is the
   most likely fix for the convergence failure.
